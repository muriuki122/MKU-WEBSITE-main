Our Research Mission

The Center for Placental Research is dedicated to advancing the understanding of placental biology in maternal and fetal health. We decode the complex mechanisms driving placental malaria and host–pathogen interactions.

Global & Local Impact

We investigate how infectious diseases influence pregnancy outcomes in resource-limited settings. Our integrated lab-to-field evidence informs next-gen diagnostics and life-saving interventions.

Training the Future

Committed to strengthening research capacity, we mentor the next generation of African scientists to lead locally grounded, globally relevant scientific discoveries.

About the Placenta

Facts and insights about the organ at the centre of maternal and child health.

01

A Lifeline Organ

The placenta transfers oxygen and nutrients, removes waste, and supports fetal growth throughout pregnancy.

02

An Immune Interface

It helps balance maternal immune protection with fetal tolerance, making it central to infection and inflammation research.

03

A Health Signal

Placental function can reflect risks such as malaria-related disease, fetal growth restriction, and other pregnancy complications.

Current Research Focus

Cutting-edge projects driving maternal health breakthroughs

Placental Malaria

Placental malaria occurs when P. falciparum-infected red blood cells accumulate in the placenta, contributing to inflammation, low birthweight, preterm birth, and maternal anaemia.

What is known

Placental infection disrupts nutrient exchange and immune balance at the maternal-fetal interface.

What remains unknown

The cellular pathways that determine why some pregnancies develop severe disease need deeper placental-specific study.

Explore placental malaria research →

Congenital Syphilis

Congenital syphilis research is framed through the placenta because maternal infection can cross the placental barrier and affect fetal development. Our diagnostics focus on earlier detection and treatment during pregnancy.
